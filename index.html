About

I am a doctoral student in the Department of English and Comparative Literature at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill. Alongside my research, I serve as the Graduate Manager of Digital Humanities in the Digital Literacy and Communications Lab. My work frequently utilizes computational text analysis and digital archiving to bridge classical rhetorical theory with contemporary online ecologies.

Research

My primary research centers on participatory discourse in online communities and memetic rhetorics as a ludic form of discourse. By drawing on affect theory and traditional rhetorical topoi, I examine the mechanics of how digital publics circulate meaning, adapt to rhetorical situations, and construct arguments.

Publications & Projects

  • Play, Rhetoric, and the Circulation of J.D. Vance Photoshops (Co-authored, June 2025)

Teaching

As a graduate instructor, I teach English 105: English Composition and Rhetoric. My pedagogy emphasizes multimedia integration—incorporating tools like Audacity for audio editing—and rigorous rhetorical audience adaptation across both the natural and social sciences.
